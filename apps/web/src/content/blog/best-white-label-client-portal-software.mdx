White-label client portal software removes the vendor's branding and replaces it with yours. For agencies and consulting firms positioning themselves as premium service providers, this is a meaningful differentiator — clients see your brand at every touchpoint, not a third-party tool you happen to subscribe to.

This guide reviews the best white-label client portal options in 2026, with a focus on how deep the branding customisation actually goes.

## What "white-label" means in practice

Full white-labelling typically includes:

- **Logo replacement** — your logo where the vendor's normally sits
- **Colour theming** — your brand colours applied to buttons, accents, and UI elements
- **"Powered by" removal** — no badge indicating which software you're using
- **Custom domain** — the portal lives at `clients.youragency.com`, not `app.vendor.com`

Not all tools offer all four. Some restrict custom domains to higher-tier plans, or charge separately for badge removal. It's worth checking the specifics before committing.

## Does white-labelling actually matter?

For agencies pitching to mid-market and enterprise clients: yes, meaningfully. A branded portal in a proposal — "we'll set up a Northwave Digital client portal for your project" — positions you differently from agencies sending weekly email updates or sharing raw Jira links.

For freelancers with small clients: less so. The free tier of a portal tool without custom branding still looks more professional than a status email. Custom branding becomes important when it's part of your pitch or when clients are paying for premium service.

## The custom domain question

Custom domains are the highest-effort, highest-impact white-label feature. They require DNS configuration and are typically restricted to higher-priced plans.

If having your portal at `portal.youragency.com` is important to you, SuiteDash or Clinked are the clearest current options. Salkaro Portal has it on the roadmap.

For most agencies starting out, logo + colour + badge removal covers the branding bases without the DNS complexity.
